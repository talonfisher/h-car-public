What is H-CAR?

H-CAR is a smoke detector for your body. It learns your personal baseline from your Fitbit and flags sustained changes over days. It names a change, never a cause. It never tells you why something changed, only that it did.

Like a smoke detector, H-CAR warns early, rarely cries wolf, and makes sure someone who can act hears it. It watches quietly in the background, day after day, learning what is normal for you.

The Smoke Detector for Your Body

Your body has a normal. H-CAR helps you act when it shifts. It is a smoke detector for the body, not a diagnosis machine. It learns from heart rate, HRV, oxygen, temperature, breathing, sleep and movement, and it names a change, never a cause.

The mission: be the smoke detector that saves lives. Three jobs: warn early, rarely cry wolf, and be heard by someone who can act.

A single bad night means nothing. A single high heart rate means nothing. But when three or more signals move away from your baseline for days in a row, that is worth noticing. H-CAR notices it, so you can act.

Your 6 Signals

H-CAR tracks six signals from your Fitbit, every day:

Resting Heart RateYour heart rate at rest, measured in beats per minute. A sustained rise can signal strain.
Heart Rate VariabilityThe variation between heartbeats, measured in milliseconds. Lower HRV often means your body is under stress.
Blood Oxygen (SpO2)Your blood oxygen saturation, measured as a percentage. Sustained drops are worth noticing.
Breathing RateYour breaths per minute while sleeping. Changes can reflect respiratory or systemic strain.
Sleep DurationHow long you sleep each night, in minutes. Shorter or disrupted sleep affects recovery.
Skin TemperatureYour skin temperature while sleeping, relative to your baseline. Deviations can signal change.

It Learns You, Not an Average

The core question H-CAR asks every day: did your numbers change more than the situation explains? Six steps:

  1. Days 1-28 learn your normal. A 15-day baseline roughly tripled false alarms (about 1.1% vs 0.32%), so 28 days it is.
  2. Anchor it, never rolling. A rolling 90-day baseline caught only 3.1% of slow declines at Act. An anchored baseline caught about 100%.
  3. Context classifies, never rewrites the score. Scores are computed first. Then context (steps, check-in answers) can file a flag as explained. No check-in answer means Unknown, which escalates like Unexplained.
  4. Judge the unexplained remainder with a robust ruler. Six signals, each in its deterioration direction. Median plus MAD, deviation capped around 4, minimum spread floor so one wild night cannot drag the baseline around.
  5. Two tiers, only Act is an alarm. Watch: 3 or more of 6 signals off, 2 consecutive days, silent log plus check-in. Act: 3 consecutive qualifying days, still worsening, at least one strong signal.
  6. A human confirms any new normal. Automatic re-baselining was tested and rejected. If your normal genuinely changes, a person confirms it.

28 Days to Learn Your Baseline

When you first connect your Fitbit, H-CAR spends 28 days learning what is normal for you. It calculates your personal median and typical variation for each signal. This is your baseline.

During these 28 days, H-CAR raises no flags. It is still learning. After day 28, monitoring begins. Your baseline continues to update slowly over time, adapting as your normal changes. Overnight is when baseline speaks clearest.

Your Baseline: Normal Variation

Normal variation around your baseline. Small ups and downs are expected. H-CAR learns how much variation is normal for you.

Watch: A Change Worth Noticing

When 3 or more signals move away from your baseline for 2 days in a row, H-CAR raises a Watch flag. This is a quiet log entry with a morning check-in. It does not alert anyone else. It is a note to pay attention.

Watch Flag Example

Multiple signals rising together for 2 days triggers a Watch. H-CAR logs it and asks you a few questions in the morning.

Act: A Strong, Sustained Change

When a Watch continues for 3 days and gets stronger, with 4 or more signals deviating or one signal moving sharply, H-CAR raises an Act flag. This is the signal that reaches another human, a person you trust, so they can check in on you.

Act Flag Example

A strong, sustained change over 3 days triggers an Act flag. This is when H-CAR alerts your trusted contact.

Privacy by Design

H-CAR uses edge-first processing. Your baseline is calculated on your device. Your raw Fitbit data stays on your phone when possible. Only flag summaries and daily heartbeats reach the server.

Your data is never sold. You can delete your account and data at any time.

The Six Rules

  • Rule 1: Anchored baseline. 99.5-100% of slow declines caught, vs 50.1% (rolling 90-day) and 3.1% at Act.
  • Rule 2: Episode lockout. No learning during an episode plus 14 clean days after. Clean-days-only learning inflated false alerts to about 16%, rejected.
  • Rule 3: Two tiers, one alarm. Synthetic healthy false alerts: 0.32% Watch, 0.002% Act. LifeSnaps first Watch rate: 1.35 per person-year.
  • Rule 4: Context-adjusted detection. Altitude 2,100 m: Watch alerts fell from 97.4% to 0.4% with context. Training block: 62.2% to 0.03%. (Simulation-tested design intent, not built in detector v2, which classifies after scoring.)
  • Rule 5: Human-confirmed re-baselining. No automatic new normal.
  • Rule 6: Real-data hardening (Nov 2026). Median + MAD, cap around 4, spread floors, no Act on thin data, 14-day merging, explained context. LifeSnaps Watch fell from 7 to 5 episodes, 3 unexplained, 0.6 per person-year, Act preserved.

Three Response Levels

  • Level 1: Watch and check. Silent log. Two days drifting. Morning summary, check-in, rest, hydrate, log.
  • Level 2: Reach out. Message a trusted contact, share location, call a clinician with your data, show Medical ID and medications.
  • Level 3: Act now. One-tap emergency call, auto-alert care circle plus location, SOS screen, pre-set countdown only if enabled.

What H-CAR Cannot Detect

H-CAR cannot detect sudden cardiac arrest, stroke as it happens, any acute event unfolding in minutes, or any event with no preceding wearable-visible change (flagged at chance level in blind tests). It cannot determine any cause or diagnosis.

The limit is structural: one score per day from daily summaries. H-CAR is built for slow sustained change over days. Quiet means no sustained drift was detected, never "nothing is wrong."

Do not wait for H-CAR. Call 911.

How H-CAR Differs from Apple, Google, and Garmin

Native watch ecosystems focus on the individual wearer. H-CAR focuses on who hears about a change and what happens before paging: a Context Check and ask-before-paging step, a user-chosen care circle, and a design where only Act reaches another human.

One platform first; cross-platform is post-proof roadmap.

Watch Tier: A Silent Log, Not an Alarm

Rule: 3 or more of 6 signals directionally off baseline, plus a combined-deviation threshold, for 2 consecutive days. Uses median + MAD, deviation capped around 4, spread floor.

  • No buzz, no page. It appears in a morning check-in.
  • A check-in answer plus step count can file it as explained.
  • Thin-data days can log Watch but never reach Act.
  • A re-flag within 14 days is the same episode.
  • LifeSnaps: 1.35 down to 0.6 unexplained per person-year after fixes.
  • Simulation: 99.1% of strong drifts caught, 41.0% of half-strength.

How Detection Works, in Plain English

  1. Compare each signal to your personal band.
  2. Smooth with a 3-day rolling average.
  3. Count how many signals agree.
  4. Grade by duration and distance from baseline.
  5. Freeze baseline updates during a deviation.
  6. Always show which signals contributed.

This page shows the model, not the recipe. The full formal spec with exact tuning constants stays in private trial and patent files. The model is described here so you can discuss it with a clinician.

Scoring Model

Six daily signals: resting heart rate, HRV, SpO2, nightly temperature deviation, sleep duration, full-sleep breathing rate. Each has one deterioration direction.

Per-signal deviation score vs your own baseline median plus MAD, with a minimum spread floor, capped around 4. Needs enough baseline observations to be reliable.

Joint score: count of signals off plus a combined-deviation measure. No per-signal weights (weighting was tried in a later version, changed no outcome on real data, not adopted). Healthy-direction movement pulls the combined measure down.

Worked example: +18 bpm vs +0.4 degrees score differently per person, depending on their own spread. A very steady baseline flags smaller absolute changes, hence the spread floor.

One Temporal Model

Exactly one scoring grain: one score per day, computed each morning from daily summaries. Two windows:

  • Acute: Watch after a short run, Act after a longer still-worsening run.
  • Sustained/episode: 14-day lockout freezes baseline learning only. Episodes separated by 14 days or less merge for paging counts.

Hour-level durations in demo scenarios are narrative illustration, retired as detector rules.

Sensitivity Modes

Balanced is the exact blind-trial parameter set, the only mode the 40-person trial will run, frozen. Gentle and Sensitive are defined but experimental, never replayed, must be labelled experimental. Switching mid-episode does not rescore.

Missing-Data Policy

No imputation. Too few signals means a gap day (not healthy), which breaks a Watch run. Thin days can log Watch but never trigger Act. Intermittent SpO2 contributes only when valid. Silence is a gap, never an all-clear.

Edge-first: the server gets only episode summaries, gap counts, and a daily heartbeat. A missed heartbeat means a gap. A short baseline extends the learning window by the shortfall.

Honest limit: the blind-trial generator had no missing-data patterns, so the policy carries no synthetic detection numbers. It is first tested in the 40-person trial.

Context and the New Default

Scores first, context second. No check-in answer means Unknown, which escalates exactly like Unexplained. Ask-before-paging: a direct contact attempt with the wearer first at Act.

Correction: the built detector does NOT pre-correct for altitude or training (that is simulation and design intent). The absolute oxygen floor is specified in structure, but the numeric value is not set and it is not in the built detector.

Layer 3 Rules (Device Safety, Demo Only)

  • Fall and rhythm accepted only from the watch platform's own cleared or validated features.
  • No SOS from a single rhythm notification (at most a prompt to sit down and repeat the device's guided reading).
  • Fall path stands alone: visible countdown, nothing sent if the wearer responds, auto-escalation only if enabled in advance.
  • Rhythm plus fall is NOT a combined rule.
  • A deliberate SOS tap needs no threshold.
  • All device safety events are logged separately from Watch and Act episodes.

Allowed and Not Allowed Claims

Allowed: Balanced (detector v2) passed all four pre-set gates of the blind validation trial on fresh seed, 12,000 synthetic people, with the confounder weakness (about 29% reaching Act) reported plainly.

Not allowed: Gentle/Sensitive tested claims, clinical detection claims, claims built on demo scenario durations, or suggestions that synthetic results prove real-body performance.

Four Hardware Paths

  1. Software on existing watches: do first. Cheapest and fastest. The prototype path.
  2. Dev board + sensors: best next step. nRF52840 plus PPG/IMU/temp modules, about $80-250 in parts.
  3. Custom wearable: later if justified. PCB, enclosure, 20-50 units, safety and radio certification.
  4. Custom chip: not yet, probably not soon. Millions and years.

Algorithms and Limitations

Method: anchored all-history baseline after 28-day learning, median + MAD, cap around 4, spread floor. Episode lockout plus 14-day merging. Scores first, then context classification. Exertion tagging built; altitude/training pre-correction is design intent, not built. Unknown escalates as unexplained. Missing data never imputed; thin days can log Watch, never Act. Watch is a silent log; Act is the only human alert, after a direct contact attempt. Human-confirmed re-baselining only.

Limitations: PPG is noisy with motion, tattoos, cold, or loose fit. HRV varies by device algorithm, not comparable across brands. Watch sleep staging is approximate. Correlation is not cause. Thresholds are experimental, zero clinical validation. LifeSnaps replay (52 people, 5.2 person-years) measured false alarms only. Rare or serious conditions may produce no wearable signal. A "stable" reading never rules out illness.

System Architecture (4 Stages)

  1. Sense: existing watch or future pod (PPG, IMU, temp, ECG spot-checks, plus symptom logs).
  2. Phone, on-device first: cleaning, quality flags, baseline model, deviation detection. Raw data stays local by default.
  3. Explain: what changed, when, contributing signals, confidence. Assistant grounded in those numbers.
  4. Act and share, only with consent: options from self-check to trusted contact to clinician to emergency, report export, care-circle alerts. Optional encrypted cloud sync for backup and multi-device, never required for core detection.

Studies 1-4 (Summary)

  • Study 1 (400 people, 150 days): strong drift 100/100 flagged, median 7 days early; half-strength 41/100; no-drift event 1/100 (chance); illness/training 88/100 by loose rule (specificity problem motivating two tiers plus context).
  • Study 2 (1,000,000 people, 150 days): strong drift 99.1% Watch / 92.5% Act; half-strength 41.0%/9.0%; silent events 0.31%/about 0%; illness 88.7%/36.1%; healthy false alerts 0.32%/0.002%. Honest note: the first scaled run collapsed to about 15% detection because synthetic drift leaked into the baseline window, caught via disagreement with Study 1, fixed and re-run.
  • Study 3 (600,000 people, full year): healthy false alerts: expanding baseline 0.24%, fixed 28-day 0.95%, rolling 90-day 0.27%. Rolling failed slow declines 50.1%/3.1%. 15-day baseline roughly tripled false alarms (about 1.1% vs 0.32%).
  • Study 4 (40,000/group, full year): anchored plus lockout: slow declines 99.8% Watch / about 100% Act (median 64/48 days early), fast drift 99.1%/92.4%, healthy false alerts 0.32%/about 0%. Failed attempt kept on record: clean-days-only learning inflated false alerts to about 16%.

Real Data Replays (PMData)

PMData (15 people, 4.04 person-years): 6 Watch flags, about 1.5 per person-year. Zero Act flags.

Honest Limits

Detection comes from invented people and physiology. LifeSnaps measures false alarms only. Whether real deterioration is preceded by multi-signal drift is an open empirical question only a prospective clinician-adjudicated study can answer. Overfitting risk with only 5.2 person-years (hence the dual gate).

H-CAR detects change, does not diagnose, predict, or replace a professional. It can never say "you are fine." The defensible claim is the "see a doctor" claim. Context corrections use population averages (individuals 60-140%). Altitude acclimatisation takes 1-3 weeks. Simulation true normals never legitimately changed outside designed scenarios.

Unresolved Technical Questions (9)

  1. HRV and sleep data exposure per platform and frequency.
  2. Deviation thresholds per person balancing early warning vs alarm fatigue.
  3. Baselines vs medication changes and ageing (no population-average correction exists).
  4. Whether consumer SpO2 can be trusted enough to weight.
  5. On-device vs cloud split for battery and privacy.
  6. Which claims keep H-CAR in "wellness" territory legally in Canada and the US.
  7. Numeric absolute oxygen floor (value still to be set with clinician input).
  8. Missing-data policy untested until the 40-person trial (blind-trial generator had no missing-data patterns).
  9. Clinical adjudication: who reviews Act episodes and trial outcomes.
